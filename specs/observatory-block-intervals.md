## Clarifications

### Q1. What exactly is the requested metric and lower-bound inclusion rule?

**Answer — resolved.** Exactly three windows, in requested order: 1 week / 24 hours / 1 hour, meaning 604800 / 86400 / 3600 seconds before a single finite batch-start wall instant T. No mandatory session metric. Select the latest indexed E at or before T and latest level-ordered floor S at or before each cutoff C, constrained to E.level. Equality includes the exact cutoff endpoint as S; the completed intervals end in (C,E.timestamp]. The entire interval crossing C counts without weighting. Mean is `(E.timestamp-S.timestamp)/(E.level-S.level)`; two blocks one level apart mean one interval. Long stalls, protocol changes and skipped browser observations do not change that denominator. Research Q9's lack of an explorer convention is superseded by this explicit validated product choice, not an unresolved research question. Source: intake Arithmetic 1–7; research Q9.

### Q2. When does missing history justify partial coverage, and can a malformed floor use the earliest row?

**Answer — resolved.** Only an empty floor array enables the one shared earliest-available read for that network batch. A usable earliest S newer than the affected cutoff may yield a partial available history mean with actual coverage disclosed. It cannot repair malformed nonempty rows, unavailable E, contradictory endpoint order or a different provider. S=E, zero/reversed span or n=0 gives insufficient data; missing/contradictory endpoints give unavailable. E at or before C yields no sample for that window, without moving its cutoff backward. A partial value does not prove genesis or complete intervening history. Source: intake Arithmetic 3–8 and Bounded acquisition.

### Q3. Which checkout, interface names and existing contracts govern integration?

**Answer — resolved.** Root source HEAD e1ca61ecfd6aef3c28a567cb29cb16ae245654da has foundation-shaped interfaces and inline prepare; the separate immutable V3 worktree checkpoint a97efb3f56c8b76d2ce1e88d1c05719a3e37992d has `productionFixture.ts` and `OBSERVATORY_TEST_PORT`. These paths are revision-qualified, not interchangeable. Implementers must reread an exact supplied integrated V3 handoff and preserve existing accepted 323 claims, including foundation and V3 contracts. TzKT REST historical spans are distinct from TzKT RPC streams, source comparison (whose 60-second stale rule remains separate), consensus authority and protocol countdowns. No generic transport infrastructure or new dependency is requested. Source: intake Architecture/Scope; spec-researcher source limit and separately verified checkpoint.

### Q4. Does each refresh publish one coherent batch or merge fresh windows with older successful windows?

**Answer — resolved from the bounded retention contract.** Shared head invalidity, explicit identity contradiction or fixed-E re-read mismatch invalidates the entire attempt; normal later indexed-head advance does not. After a valid shared E and consistency check, individual window failures remain independently represented in the new three-window batch. Do not silently fill a failed current window from a previous T: the retained object is the latest committed three-window batch, with shared as-of provenance. An older committed result retained after a failed batch must remain attributed stale/historical with its original facts. No network result overwrites another. Source: intake Arithmetic 8, Bounded acquisition/consistency, lifecycle retention.

### Q5. Which exact receipt starts the 120-second metric freshness age?

**Answer — resolved by root decision, 2026-10-15 06:35:54 UTC.** Metric freshness age zero is the successful batch completion receipt, captured when final successful consistency/read work completes, before rendering. Preserve original batch T, batch-start wall/monotonic receipts and metadata's own response receipts separately. Stale begins at >=120 seconds since completion receipt, or immediately upon interruption/invalidation. Rendering, navigation and selection cannot reset age or clear sticky staleness. Last indexed-block age remains separate. The earlier clarifier proposal offered completion receipt; root explicitly accepted and specified the capture point. Source: intake Arithmetic 1 and Refresh/freshness; actual root decision.

### Q6. What happens to a queued eligible trigger when completion occurs before the next allowed start, or resume happens inside cooldown?

**Answer — resolved by root decision, 2026-10-15 06:35:54 UTC.** Drop cooldown-ineligible triggers without a deferred automatic start or timer. Initial/resume also respects >=60 seconds between starts. At most one newest eligible trigger may queue during an active batch; it may start upon completion only if collection remains allowed and start spacing is then >=60 seconds. Otherwise clear it until a new eligible material-head event, allowed resume or explicit Refresh. A visible cooldown countdown may tick without initiating reads. The earlier clarifier proposal offered this event-driven policy; root accepted and made clearing explicit. Source: intake Refresh/lifecycle; actual root decision.

### Q7. Which material applied-head changes and context invalidations trigger refresh or sticky staleness?

**Answer — resolved by root decision, 2026-10-15 06:35:54 UTC.** A refresh trigger requires actually accepted changed immutable applied-head identity/facts, including same-height changed hash/verified round, level advance, skipped-level gaps or rollback. Exact repeats and timer estimates do not qualify. Proven reorganization/rollback or explicit contradiction to the metric network/source identity immediately marks retained results stale. Ordinary advance or RPC provider rotation within the same confirmed network does not invalidate an independently attributed TzKT historical span; RPC-versus-TzKT height difference alone is not an identity contradiction. Pause, hidden, pagehide and stop always abort, clear pending, advance generation and stale retained data. The earlier clarifier proposal suggested this distinction; root accepted and explicitly qualified accepted facts, verified rounds and height-difference interpretation. Source: intake Refresh/lifecycle; research Q2; actual root decision.

### Q8. What operational and presentation limits are binding versus unproved external assumptions?

**Answer — resolved.** Per network: maximum 6 GET reads (1 E + 3 floors + optional shared earliest + 1 fixed-level E verification), maximum 2 concurrent, each full-body read <=8 seconds, whole batch <=24 seconds and each streamed body <=16 KiB; failures count and ignored abort must still settle. Use existing network REST base, anonymous omitted credentials, no-store, redirect error, no retries/pagination/fallback/extra metadata read. Lag uses coherent same-response known/indexed metadata, never temporally filtered E.level. Display visible three values, actual coverage and linked TzKT attribution; endpoint hashes/formula/exact receipts may use native disclosure. Preserve accessibility, focus/scroll/selection and accepted responsive/theme/engine matrix. Existing live probes prove only small responses at their recorded instant, not historical completeness or availability. Source: intake Bounded acquisition, Refresh and Presentation; research Q8; spec-researcher comparison workflow limits.

## Challenges


**C-1 [US-1, scenario 5 — outcome taxonomy].** The scenario combines “insufficient data/unavailable,” while intake Arithmetic 6 calls zero/negative duration and disagreeing endpoint order insufficient data, then calls contradictory endpoints unavailable. Clarification Q2 repeats both categories. Exactly which result/reason applies to positive level difference with negative time difference, reversed levels, equal time with different levels, and E at/before C? The final spec needs one determinate classification for each rather than an implementer-selected slash label.

**C-2 [US-1, scenarios 3–5 — source rows and ordering].** What is the required outcome for a limit=1 response containing two rows, a floor S above E.level, a floor timestamp after C, or an earliest row at/before C after the floor returned empty? These violate different selection assertions; specify whether each invalidates only its affected window or the shared batch. An earliest response is shared, so its failure scope is otherwise unclear.

**C-3 [US-1, scenarios 1/6; US-2, scenario 3 — timestamp equality and precision].** Guards accept timezone offsets, and the metric supports subsecond intervals. Does fixed-E “exact timestamp” equality compare the represented instant or the source string (e.g. Z versus +00:00)? What timestamp precision is supported, and what happens to extra precision accepted by parsing but truncated in arithmetic? Calendar validity alone does not settle equivalent encodings or precision loss.

**C-4 [US-1, scope and scenarios 4/5 — explicit provider history evidence].** Intake Arithmetic 8 requires unavailable/partial with a specific reason when TzKT exposes missing/partial history, but neither stories nor Q8 identify the admissible evidence or its effect on an otherwise valid floor pair. Which response facts qualify, and when does a valid endpoint-only result become partial versus unavailable? Absence of independently checked intermediate history is already an accepted limitation and must not itself become a new failure test.

**C-5 [US-2, scenarios 3–5 — failure scope and deadlines].** An invalid/missing fixed-E recheck prevents commitment, but what does a timeout, HTTP failure, invalid JSON or oversized body on that mandatory recheck do? Conversely, if a floor times out at 8 seconds but remaining reads finish within 24 seconds, may the coherent new batch commit with that window unavailable? Define whole-batch deadline behavior when some windows already succeeded; “acquisition settles as failure” and independent window failure currently leave different permitted outcomes.

**C-6 [US-2, scenario 6 — metadata provenance selection].** Several bounded responses can contain distinct coherent known/indexed metadata and receipts. Which response supplies the displayed lag/synchronization evidence? If initial metadata is coherent but later recheck metadata is missing/contradictory, can the earlier observation remain displayed with its original receipt? Same-response arithmetic is settled, but response selection and malformed synchronized-at handling are not.

**C-7 [US-2, scenarios 1/5 — identity contradiction].** Which actually supplied identity facts can establish an explicit network contradiction, given the minimal block projection and optional headers? What happens to an unrecognized or malformed identity assertion, as opposed to a recognized contradictory one? This must remain distinct from RPC/TzKT height difference, which Q7 explicitly says is not a contradiction; no additional identity read is authorized.

**C-8 [US-3, scenarios 3–5 — invalidation during an active batch].** Q7 makes a proven reorganization/rollback immediately stale retained results, but does it also fence an already running pre-event attempt? Can that attempt later commit and clear sticky invalidation merely because its fixed-E recheck still matches? Specify which post-invalidation successful batch may clear the stale state. Pause/hidden generation fencing is explicit; reorganization/context invalidation fencing is not.

**C-9 [US-3, scenarios 1/2 — Refresh ownership].** Both networks collect independently while tab selection is presentation only. Does the native Refresh block intervals action target the displayed network, both networks, or a separately named network? Specify cooldown/disabled feedback and concurrent-action behavior when one network is cooling down or active and the other is eligible.

**C-10 [US-3, scenarios 1/3 — refresh/failure reader states].** When an attempt runs over an existing result, does the reader retain that result with a loading annotation, and what receipt/failure information remains visible after failure? A failed whole attempt retains the old batch stale/historical; an independently failed window in a successfully committed batch cannot inherit an old value (Q4). Define the displayed state when no previous batch exists, including paused/initially hidden collection, without treating an absent value as zero.

**C-11 [US-3, scenario 3 — independent indexed-block age].** Completion age is now settled, but what precisely anchors indexed-block age when E is historical and wall time jumps during acquisition or after commitment? Specify its baseline receipt and whether clock inconsistency can produce unavailable age or a clamped value. A wall jump must not silently alter the mean, T or completion-based freshness.

**C-12 [US-3, scenario 6 — reviewable visual contract].** The scenario says “accepted” dimensions/zoom/contrast without a revision-qualified matrix or numeric contrast reference. The researcher establishes that root and V3 checkouts differ. Which exact existing foundation/V3 acceptance interfaces supply that matrix for this additive region, and which new reader checks demonstrate preservation at those sizes? This requests a determinate contract/reference, not execution evidence at specification time.

## Required prior-art divergence challenges

These divergences have validated product rationale in the intake. They must be explicitly dispositioned against that rationale, rather than reopened as unknown arithmetic or infrastructure choices.

**C-13 [US-2 — TzKT blocks REST].** Prior art: TzKT provides filtered, level-sorted bounded lists with pagination and exact-or-lower timestamp lookup ([Blocks controller](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/baking-bad/tzkt/master/Tzkt.Api/Controllers/BlocksController.cs)); stories assume endpoint-only floor queries with no pagination and no intermediate-block verification—justify divergence/adopt prior approach. In particular, what stated evidence limitation prevents the bounded selection from being advertised as fully verified historical continuity? The intake already selects bounded endpoints and explicit indexed-span attribution; the final spec must preserve that justification.

**C-14 [US-2 — Octez historical RPC].** Prior art: Octez offers historical hash fragments with operator-dependent retention/context ([Shell RPC reference](https://octez.tezos.com/docs/shell/rpc.html), [history modes](https://octez.tezos.com/docs/user/history_modes.html)); stories assume TzKT-only historical reads with no RPC fallback—justify divergence/adopt prior approach. The accepted bounded browser-only source contract must explain why unavailable TzKT history remains unavailable rather than asserting archival node coverage.

**C-15 [US-1 — Tenderbake timing].** Prior art: Tenderbake defines individual timestamp differences and round-dependent protocol duration ([Consensus reference](https://octez.tezos.com/docs/active/consensus.html#time-between-blocks)); stories assume a fixed-wall-window telescoping timestamp/level mean including the full crossing interval—justify divergence/adopt prior approach. The validated empirical-versus-protocol distinction must remain explicit so neither nominal delay nor unfinished current wait is substituted into the formula.

**C-16 [US-1/US-3 — TzIndex health].** Prior art: TzIndex computes weighted consensus penalties with crawler synchronization gating ([Tip implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blockwatch-cc/tzindex/master/server/explorer/tip.go)); stories assume arithmetic means and separate source/chain evidence without a health score—justify divergence/adopt prior approach. The final criteria must make the accepted separation falsifiable, including provider failure and a freshly fetched old block.

**C-17 [US-3 — Node monitoring].** Prior art: Octez monitoring scrapes instantaneous metrics into configurable retained time series ([Monitoring](https://octez.tezos.com/docs/user/node-monitoring.html)); stories assume event-triggered browser acquisition, no timer polling and only one retained batch—justify divergence/adopt prior approach. The accepted quiet-period behavior must not imply a continuously maintained week-long series or automatic retry after cooldown.

## Functional Requirements

- **FR-001** [US-1]: For an observer starting a batch, the metric MUST capture one finite wall T and wall/monotonic start receipts and MUST offer exactly the ordered 604800/86400/3600-second windows, with unrounded C=T-duration independent of civil-time changes. Trace: US-1/S1; AC-1.
- **FR-002** [US-1]: When selecting endpoints for an observer, the metric MUST select latest descending-level indexed E at/before T and descending-level S at/before each C, constrained to E.level; exact cutoff equality MUST qualify and the full crossing interval MUST count without weighting. Trace: US-1/S1–3; AC-1, AC-18.
- **FR-003** [US-1]: When computing a valid pair for an observer, the metric MUST retain integer milliseconds until division, calculate elapsed seconds divided by E.level−S.level, and retain the unrounded mean; n=1, irregular intervals, protocol transitions and skipped observations/large level gaps MUST preserve that denominator. Ongoing wait and protocol delay MUST NOT enter arithmetic. Trace: US-1/S1–2,6; AC-1, AC-6, AC-18.
- **FR-004** [US-1]: When only empty floor arrays lack S, the metric MUST permit at most one shared ascending-level earliest lookup bounded to E; each dependent window MUST require earliest newer than C and no later/higher than E, label a usable pair partial available history and disclose actual extent. Nonempty malformed floors MUST NOT use it; contradictory earliest-at/before-C MUST make that window unavailable; no recursive fallback is permitted. Trace: US-1/S4–5; AC-5.
